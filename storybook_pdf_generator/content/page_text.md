## Page 1:
### A cozy guide to your appetite signals
Your body has a quiet way of talking to your brain.
Two key messengers are ghrelin and leptin.
They help guide when to eat and when to pause.
> Hunger and fullness are body signals, not personal failure.

## Page 2:
### The big idea in plain words
Ghrelin is a hunger messenger.
Leptin is an energy-status messenger.
Both send updates to the brain.
- Ghrelin often says: “Time to find food.”
- Leptin often says: “We have energy stored.”
- Your brain blends both signals with habits, sleep, and stress.
> These signals work as a team.

## Page 3:
### Meet ghrelin
Ghrelin is made mostly in the stomach.
It usually rises before meals.
That rising signal can make food smell and look extra good.
- Long gaps without food can raise ghrelin.
- After eating, ghrelin usually falls.
- This helps create a natural meal rhythm.
> Ghrelin is your gentle “fuel reminder.”

## Page 4:
### Meet leptin
Leptin is made mostly by fat cells.
It tells the brain about stored energy.
Leptin supports fullness and long-term balance.
- It is not only about one meal.
- It reflects the body’s bigger energy picture.
- It helps the brain decide how much drive to eat is needed.
> Leptin is like a “storage update” for the brain.

## Page 5:
### The appetite conversation cycle
Empty stomach can raise ghrelin.
Ghrelin can increase hunger.
Eating brings nutrients and stomach stretch signals.
Satiety signals rise after and during a meal.
Over time, leptin reflects overall energy stores.
- Empty stomach → ghrelin up
- Eat meal → fullness signals up
- Longer term → leptin reports stored energy
> Short-term and long-term signals work together.

## Page 6:
### When signals get mixed up
Sleep loss can nudge hunger signals upward.
Stress can change appetite and cravings.
Highly processed foods can make stopping harder.
Repeated overeating may contribute to leptin resistance.
Constant strict dieting can disrupt natural hunger rhythms.
- This is common and changeable.
- Small habits can help rebuild signal clarity.
> Confused signals are not permanent.

## Page 7:
### Real-life patterns you might notice
Sleep-deprived day: stronger snack urges, lower fullness awareness.
Stressful day: comfort-food cravings may rise.
Balanced meal day: steadier energy and calmer hunger.
Ultra-processed snack cycle: quick hunger return.
- Protein + fiber can improve staying power.
- Slower eating gives fullness signals time.
> Your daily context changes how signals feel.

## Page 8:
### Gentle steps that support better signaling
Sleep enough most nights.
Build meals with protein, fiber, and whole foods.
Eat slowly so your brain can catch up.
Manage stress with small daily resets.
Move regularly in ways you enjoy.
Avoid extreme restriction.
Notice true hunger versus habit cravings.
> Aim for progress, not perfection.
